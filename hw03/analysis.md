# HW3 Analysis: Do Executive Changes Precede or Follow Earnings?

Across the five companies, executive changes lean toward coming *before* an earnings announcement rather than after one: of the 16 Item 5.02 filings that reported a departure or appointment (30 individual events), 11 were filed closest to an upcoming earnings date, 4 closest to a recent one, and 1 in the same week (22, 6, and 2 by event count). The "before" filings landed 10 to 42 days ahead of earnings (median 29 days), so they cluster in the second half of the quarter, with Walmart's CEO succession announced just six days before its third-quarter results. The pattern is not uniform: JPMorgan and Walmart had no changes after earnings, while Microsoft's board changes (Hoffman, Di Sibio) and the NVIDIA and Apple filings in December and July fell 15 to 43 days after a report. Because earnings arrive roughly every 90 days, an even before/after split is what we would expect if timing were unrelated to the earnings calendar, so the skew is suggestive, but with only five companies, 16 filings, and multi-person announcements (Walmart's January filing alone names five people) it is not strong enough to call a rule. One plausible reading is that companies try to settle leadership changes before reporting results, but this data cannot show intent.
